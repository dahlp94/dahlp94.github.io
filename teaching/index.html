Teaching Philosophy

My goal in teaching mathematics and statistics is to help students move beyond memorizing procedures and toward understanding why a method works, when it should be used, and what its result means.

I try to build that understanding by connecting formal ideas to concrete problems, making assumptions explicit, and encouraging students to explain their reasoning rather than focusing only on obtaining the final answer. In statistics especially, I want students to see computation as part of a larger process that includes asking a clear question, choosing an appropriate method, evaluating uncertainty, and interpreting the result in context.

I also recognize that students enter quantitative courses with different levels of preparation and confidence. I aim to make expectations clear, break difficult ideas into manageable steps, and create opportunities for students to ask questions and develop confidence through practice.
